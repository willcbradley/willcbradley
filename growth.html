Growth

We've come a long way.

Over thousands of years, humanity has cured incurable diseases, and produced incredible art, and lifted billions of people out of poverty.

It hasn't all been good, though.

As things get better, we get complacent. Yesterday's work creates room for corruption today. So if we aren't careful, it becomes tempting to cut out the middle man and conclude that growth is harmful.

But this conclusion is exactly wrong.

Corruption is an inevitable side effect of growth. We fix it with more growth. More earnest effort pointed at important problems. More people focused on making things better, as broadly as possible, over the longest time horizon they can imagine.

There's no upper limit. If you gave a caveman an iPhone, he'd try to eat it. We're constrained by our knowledge, not by some finite set of expendable resources.

And what's the alternative? We wait for extinction? We mask our resentment as compassion, and try to tear everything down? That's no alternative at all.

Who will our kids look up to? How will they look up at all, if they're too demoralised to believe that "up" exists?

Look around. The world is full of suffering. Everywhere I turn, I see people in pain. It's not good enough. These are solvable problems. We can fix them, if we work harder.

Will Bradley
