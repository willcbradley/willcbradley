Is Anything More Important Than Work?

Work is important.

We owe our lives to the efforts of people we'll never meet. And as tech speeds up, the opportunity cost of doing anything other than working is getting harder to justify.

But other responsibilities still exist. We still have the option of getting married, and raising families, and forming communities.

Why worry about these, at the cost of work?

Responsibilities aren't free.

At minimum, they need regular time and attention. And the more they require, the less bandwidth we have left for work.

This is an age-old problem. But as time passes and the world gets faster, it takes more and more bandwidth to work on the cutting edge. So all of a sudden, non-work responsibilities involve bigger and bigger sacrifices.

Why make these sacrifices, then?

There's only so much time.

I'm optimistic about the future of medicine, but I doubt we'll solve immortality. One person, no matter how capable, can only do so much.

Why does this matter?

Because it means our work needs to outlive us. There are unlimited problems — no matter how hard we try, we'll never get through all of them. Somebody will need to pick up where we leave off.

So, we teach them how.

We commit to our imperfect careers, and our imperfect relationships, and our imperfect lives. We weigh ourselves down with responsibility, so the next generation can go farther than we will.

By all means, we still work extremely hard. But it's for a better reason.

Today's work is nothing without tomorrow.
