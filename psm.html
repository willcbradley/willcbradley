Positive-Sum Medicine

Doctors used to work on the frontier.

From antisepsis to vaccination to blood transfusion, there was a tight feedback loop between theory and practice.

Today, that loop is breaking. The frontier has splintered off into the domain of specialised, non-clinical researchers. These are highly technical people, working on highly technical problems. But they lack the tacit knowledge that comes from seeing patients.

Meanwhile, without net growth, healthcare is left to cannibalise itself. As the turf war gets more violent, we risk losing sight of the fundamentals that make medicine what it is. Over time, earnest work is increasingly stamped out by bureaucracy.

What to do, then?

Make bureaucracy irrelevant. Develop deep clinical expertise and deep technical expertise. Bridge the gap between theory and practice, without becoming dependent on all the politics in the middle.

There's a time and a place for guardrails, particularly in medicine. But their place isn't everywhere, and their time isn't always.

How many people are dying for lack of progress? How many kids without mothers? How many wives without husbands?

We'll make our own frontier.
